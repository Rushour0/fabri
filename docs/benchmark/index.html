Fabri — company evaluation Study 01 · 20 July 2026

Support HQ passed three times. Then ten runs said ninety percent.

Fabri asks a strict question: does a company of AI agents finish a real task correctly, every single time — from clean, isolated state? A three-run gate said Support HQ did. A ten-run confirmation said 90%. The other two companies never came close. Here is the honest scorecard.

The three-run gate is what fooled us. Support HQ cleared it 3/3 — a small sample. Ten runs of the same setup dropped it to 9/10, and the gate demands 100%. The larger sample didn't refine the claim; it overturned it.

Why the numbers can be trusted

  • An unfinished run earns no score. Whether the work completed and whether it was any good are measured apart, and never quietly averaged together.
  • A change that does nothing spends nothing. Candidate tweaks are bounded and rejected at preflight, so the search can't run up a bill chasing non-differences.
  • Every sub-agent's cost is counted. Static specialists and spawned children alike roll up into one total — the whole company's tree, not just the top.

The honest part

What this establishes

  • The harness catches real over-claims. Two of three failed by asserting things absent from the evidence — and the rubric flagged each one.
  • A three-run gate is statistically fragile. Support HQ's 3/3 fell to 9/10 once we ran it ten times.
  • The cost is known, recursive, and small. ~$0.02 per run; the whole three-company study cost $0.82.

What it does not

  • That any of these setups is trustworthy at the 100% bar. None cleared it. That's the finding, not a footnote.
  • That memory makes a company better. The memory-vs-control study is the next experiment.
  • That the setups can't improve. These are as-shipped. The point is to measure honestly first, then fix.
